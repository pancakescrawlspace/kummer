=========================================================================
 Does the l-primary reduction survive a UNION of reaches?
=========================================================================

-------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Failure 1.  MEMBERSHIP is not a mod-l condition.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Frattini decides whether a subgroup is EVERYTHING.  Coverage asks
 whether particular ELEMENTS lie in it, and mod l one sees only R + lG:
        abar in Rbar  in G/lG   <=>   a in R + lG,
 which is in general strictly bigger than R.  Take G = Z/4, R = 0, a = 2.
   a in R ?                      0
   a mod l                       0
   image of R in G/lG            [0]
   R + lG                        [0, 2]
   => the mod-l test PASSES on an a that is not in R.

 This one already rules out transplanting section 3.5.1 verbatim: there
 the mod-l quotient was enough because the question was 'is R all of G',
 and Frattini answers exactly that.  Coverage is a different question.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Failure 2.  The EXISTENTIAL does not commute with the layers.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Coverage is    for all (a,b),  EXISTS R,  for all l : <a_l,b_l> in R_l.
 Checking layer by layer proves the weaker
                for all (a,b),  for all l,  EXISTS R : <a_l,b_l> in R_l,
 and the entry that works may DIFFER from layer to layer.  Smallest
 instance: G = Z/6 with the two entries <3> and <2>.
   R_1 = [0, 3]   R_2 = [0, 2, 4]
   ordered pairs covered by no single entry : 24 of 36,  e.g. [0, 1] and [0, 5]
   layer l = 2 : G_l = [0, 3]   covered layerwise: 1   (always by R_1)
   layer l = 3 : G_l = [0, 2, 4]   covered layerwise: 1   (always by R_2)
   => EVERY layer is covered and the group is NOT.  Layer 2 is covered
      only by R_1 and layer 3 only by R_2, so no single entry does both.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------
 What survives, and what the ledger actually does
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
 The repair is not to drop the decomposition but to stop quantifying
 inside it.  For each layer l and each subgroup H of G_l on at most two
 generators put the MASK  m_l(H) = { R in L : H subset R_l }, a subset of
 the ledger.  Then, by the exactness of the primary decomposition,
        (a,b) is covered  <=>  intersection over l of m_l(<a_l,b_l>) is
                                nonempty,
 so coverage holds iff no choice of one mask per layer has empty
 intersection.  The layers are still used -- containment is tested in
 them -- but the existential is resolved once, at the end, over the small
 finite set L.  That is the star test of section 2.3.4.

 On Failure 2 the mask test gives the right answer:
   masks at l = 2 : [[1], [1, 2]]
   masks at l = 3 : [[2], [1, 2]]
   mask pairs with empty intersection : 1   => NOT covered, correctly

-------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Why the coupling has never yet bitten, which is data and not a theorem
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Every reach in every ledger computed in this document has index 1 or 2
 in its arena (66 of index 2, 14 of index 1, none other).  An index-2
 reach is full at every layer except l = 2, so all layers l != 2 return
 the single full mask and cannot constrain the intersection: the whole
 question collapses onto l = 2 and the coupling is vacuous.  That is why
 the prototype in section 2.3.4 reproduced the flat verdict exactly.
 It would stop being vacuous the moment one tuple carried, say, an
 index-2 reach alongside an index-3 one -- precisely the shape of
 Failure 2.  Nothing forbids that; it just has not occurred yet.
