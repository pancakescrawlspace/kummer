padic-ptorsion.gp -- where the p-torsion of arXiv:1211.5833 comes from

  (1) the criterion as a_(p-1) = 2p mod p^2, tested directly:
      p=3, coefficient a_2 : torsion at a_2 = [6] (mod 9); 2p = 6  agrees
      p=5, coefficient a_4 : torsion at a_4 = [10] (mod 25); 2p = 10  agrees
      p=7, coefficient a_6 : torsion at a_6 = [14] (mod 49); 2p = 14  agrees
      p-1 must be a Weierstrass weight, i.e. p-1 in [1, 2, 3, 4, 6], i.e. p in [2, 3, 5, 7]
      mismatches : 0 of 3

  (2) p-torsion <=> [p] kills E_0/E_1 into E_2:
      v(x(pP)) drops below -2 exactly when the congruence holds : 0 wrong of 36

  (3) p-torsion forces the inertia image to be large:
      p=5 : e with torsion [4, 6], e without [3, 4, 6];  need e >= 4 : holds
      p=7 : e with torsion [6], e without [3, 4, 6];  need e >= 6 : holds
      violations of e >= p-1 : 0
      and e <= 6 for p >= 5 (Aut of an elliptic curve), so p - 1 <= 6, p <= 7

Two independent reasons for the same list of four primes.  Arithmetically,
the criterion is a congruence on a_(p-1), and a Weierstrass equation has
no coefficient of index p-1 once p > 7.  Geometrically, the torsion point
must live in the formal group over the field where good reduction is
attained, which needs ramification at least p-1, while the inertia image
of a potentially good curve has order at most 6.  Both bounds are Aut(E).
