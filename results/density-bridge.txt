(2) THE UNTWISTED ARGUMENT IS VACUOUS ON THE SURFACE.
    In ec-density-bm.typ the obstruction pairs a point against a class:
    inv_v A_beta(Q) = <Q_v, beta_v>_v, with beta ranging over H^1(Q,E).
    On the Kummer surface the two arguments are BOTH points -- the surface
    supplies pairs (P,Q) of points on the same twist -- so the pairing is
    <delta_v P, delta_v Q>_v with both entries in the local Kummer image
    W_v.  And W_v is MAXIMAL ISOTROPIC:

      v      dim H^1(Q_v, E[2])   dim W_v = dim E(Q_v)/2   isotropic?
      3            0                     0                  yes (W^perp = W)
      5            2                     1                  yes (W^perp = W)
      7            0                     0                  yes (W^perp = W)
      11            0                     0                  yes (W^perp = W)
      23            2                     1                  yes (W^perp = W)
      37            0                     0                  yes (W^perp = W)

    dim W_v is always half of dim H^1(Q_v,E[2]) -- that is the standard
    fact used in ec-density-bm.typ section 7 to say W_v^perp = W_v.  So
    <delta_v P, delta_v Q>_v = 0 identically, at every place, and
    reciprocity gives 0 = 0.  The single-curve argument SAYS NOTHING here.

    kummer-padic-density.typ section 5.1.5 fixes this by TWISTING the
    pairing by a non-scalar phi in End_G(E[n]):
        beta_v(P,Q) = <delta_v P, phi delta_v Q>_v ,
    which needs E[n] DECOMPOSABLE (otherwise End_G(E[n]) = F_n, phi is
    scalar, and beta collapses back to the vanishing pairing).  Since phi
    is Galois-equivariant, reciprocity still gives sum_v beta_v = 0.
    That extra ingredient is the whole difference between the two settings.

(3) WHAT wild-symbols.typ CONTRIBUTES: the sharpness of level3.gp's lemma.
    level3.gp evaluates the WILD cubic symbol at 3 by the product formula,
    and needs every class of K^*/(K^*)^3, K = Q_3(zeta_3), to have a global
    representative.  Its stated reason: U^(4) is inside (K^*)^3, so a class
    is fixed by its valuation and its unit part mod 9.  Brute force over
    O/pi^12 = O/3^6 confirms it -- AND shows 4 cannot be lowered to 3.

       m    units = 1 mod pi^m    all cubes?
       2        59049             no (52488 of them fail)
       3        19683             no (13122 of them fail)
       4        6561             YES
       5        2187             YES

    At m = 3 exactly one third of the units are cubes, so mod pi^3 would
    lose a full layer.  level3.gp's mod-9 precision is exactly right and
    cannot be reduced.

(4) A CORRECTION TO ec-density-bm.typ SECTION 6.
    That section called the index-11 case 'blocked by an unimplemented wild

    round, and both have their inputs at 11.

    (a) level3.gp's product-formula evasion needs (i) a single prime above
        ell in Q(zeta_ell), and (ii) a depth bound giving every local class
        a global representative.  Both hold at 11:
          Q(zeta_3) : h = 1,  primes above 3 = 1,  e = 2,  f = 1,  depth pe/(p-1) = 3
          Q(zeta_11) : h = 1,  primes above 11 = 1,  e = 10,  f = 1,  depth pe/(p-1) = 11
        ell is totally ramified in Q(zeta_ell) for every ell, so (i) is
        automatic; (ii) is the depth bound of wild-symbols.typ.  At 11 the
        depth is 11, so U^(12) consists of 11th powers, and since
        v(11^2) = 20 >= 12 a class is pinned down modulo 121.

    (b) kummer-survey.typ section on 11a1 at p = 11 does not evaluate the
        symbol at all: the proof needs beta_11 to be NON-DEGENERATE, not to
        be computed, and split multiplicative reduction (Tate curve)
        settles that structurally -- the Tate mu_5 is a third line, distinct
        from both global subgroups, so phi restricts to an isomorphism and
        the Weil pairing of two distinct lines is perfect.

    So 'no explicit class' and 'no proof' are different things, and the
    survey has already separated them.

(5) IS THE FAILURE FOR ell > 5 A BRAUER-GROUP FACT?

    The literal claim -- 'the geometric Brauer group of a K3 has no
    ell-torsion for ell > 5' -- is FALSE.  For a K3 surface over an
    algebraically closed field of characteristic 0,
        Br(Xbar) = (Q/Z)^(22 - rho),   rho = rank NS(Xbar) <= 20,
    so Br(Xbar)[ell] = (Z/ell)^(22-rho) is non-zero for EVERY ell.

    But there is an exact true statement next to it, and it IS the reason.
    For A = E_1 x E_2 the Kunneth decomposition of H^2 gives
        Br(Abar)[ell] = Hom(E_1[ell], E_2[ell]) / (Hom(E_1,E_2) tensor F_ell),
    the quotient being the algebraic (Neron-Severi) part.  For E_1 = E_2 = E
    without CM, Hom(E,E) = Z, so
        Br(Abar)[ell] = End(E[ell]) / F_ell = M_2(F_ell) / scalars,
    and Br(Kum(A)bar) = Br(Abar) away from 2 (Skorobogatov-Zarhin).  Taking
    Galois invariants:
        Br(Xbar)^{G_Q}[ell]  =  End_G(E[ell]) / F_ell .
    So 'the twisting endomorphism phi exists' -- the survey's criterion
    End_G(E[ell]) != F_ell -- is LITERALLY 'the Kummer surface has a
    Galois-invariant transcendental Brauer class of order ell'.  The search
    for phi and the search for ell-torsion are the same search, and the
    scalars are quotiented out precisely because they are the algebraic part.

    What bounds ell is then a modular-curve theorem, not a K3 fact.
    End_G(E[ell]) != F_ell needs the mod-ell image to be small.  Over Q, for
    ODD ell, only one of the survey's four structural cases can occur:

      * two stable lines (split Cartan)  <=>  a non-cuspidal rational point
        of X_split(ell) = X_0(ell^2).  By Mazur-Kenku the cyclic isogeny
        degrees over Q are N <= 19 and N in {21,25,27,37,43,67,163}, so
        N = ell^2 forces ell^2 in {4,9,25}: ell in {2,3,5}.  ell^2 = 49 is
        NOT on the list.
      * one stable line with equal characters: chi_1 = chi_2 and
        chi_1 chi_2 = cyclotomic force chi_1^2 = chi_cyc mod ell; with a
        rational ell-torsion point (chi_1 = 1) this needs ell = 2.
      * image inside a NONSPLIT Cartan: IMPOSSIBLE over Q for odd ell.
        Complex conjugation c lies in the image, with det c = chi_cyc(c) = -1
        and c^2 = 1, so its eigenvalues are 1 and -1, distinct for odd ell.
        An element of a nonsplit Cartan acts with eigenvalues alpha,
        alpha^ell for alpha in F_{ell^2}: equal when alpha is in F_ell, and
        conjugate-not-in-F_ell otherwise -- never {1,-1}.
      * larger irreducible image: End_G = F_ell, scalar.

      So over Q the decomposable case is ell in {2,3,5} exactly.

    A caveat on the brute force below: ellisomat records isogeny targets up
    to ISOMORPHISM, so two distinct stable lines with isomorphic quotients
    register once -- 14a1 has isogeny degrees {1,2,3,6} yet psi_3 has TWO
    rational roots, hence two stable 3-lines.  The census therefore UNDER-
    counts, and the exact statement rests on Mazur-Kenku, not on it.
    Brute-force census (a lower bound on which ell occur):
      44741 curves examined;  ell with E[ell] decomposable: [2, 3, 5]

    And ell = 7 really does not occur, even on the CM curve of conductor 49:
      [1, -1, 0, -2, -1]  N = 49, j = -3375 (CM by sqrt(-7)), isogeny degrees [1, 2, 7, 14]
      -- a single 7-isogeny, not two, so E[7] is reducible but NOT
      decomposable, and End_G(E[7]) is scalar.

    Conclusion: the intuition is right, the statement needs repair.  The
    obstruction to going past ell = 5 is not that a K3 has no ell-torsion in
    its geometric Brauer group -- it always does -- but that the
    GALOIS-INVARIANT part vanishes, and that vanishing is Mazur-Kenku.

(6) DOES beta_p DEPEND ON THE TWIST d, OR ONLY ON ITS CLASS AT p?

    Only on the class -- and the reason is a triviality once stated.  If
    d/d' is a square in Q_p then
        psi : E_d -> E_d',   (x,y) -> (c^2 x, c^3 y),  c^2 = d/d' ,
    is an ISOMORPHISM DEFINED OVER Q_p.  It carries E_d[ell] to E_d'[ell],
    each Galois-stable line to the corresponding one (so it commutes with
    phi), and preserves the Weil pairing.  Hence it identifies W_p with W_p'
    and beta_p with beta_p'.  Same form, on the nose.  ONE CHECK PER SQUARE
    CLASS suffices -- four at odd p, eight at 2 -- not one per twist.

    E = 14a1 (E[3] decomposable: psi_3 has two rational roots), ell = 3,
    critical place p = 7.  Class [1] = the 7-adic units d = 1,2,4 mod 7:

       d      Kodaira@7   c_7   a_7   M = c_7(7-a_7)
     1          7        3     1        18
     2          7        3     1        18
     4          7        3     1        18
     8          7        3     1        18
     9          7        3     1        18
     11          7        3     1        18
     15          7        3     1        18
     22          7        3     1        18
     23          7        3     1        18

     247          7        3     1        18
     -6          7        3     1        18
     -5          7        3     1        18

    and a NON-square class at 7 (d = 3,5,6 mod 7), for contrast:
       d      Kodaira@7   c_7   a_7   M
     3          7        1     -1        8
     5          7        1     -1        8

    Identical within a class, different across classes.

    What DOES move with d is the vanishing AWAY from p.  beta_v can be
    non-zero only where dim W_v = 2, which needs mu_ell in Q_v, i.e.
    v = 1 mod ell.  The set of such bad places is exactly the d-dependent
    part of the argument:

       d        bad primes of E_d          of those, = 1 mod 3
     1      [2, 3, 7]       []
     2      [2, 3, 7]       []
     4      [2, 3, 7]       []
     11      [2, 3, 7, 11]       []
     15      [2, 3, 5, 7]       []
     23      [2, 3, 7, 23]       []
     37      [2, 3, 7, 37]       [37]
     247      [2, 3, 7, 13, 19]       [13, 19]
     -5      [2, 3, 5, 7]       []

    d = 37 and d = 247 each bring in a new place where beta could survive.
    THAT is the family-uniform obligation, and it is why the CM table lists
    'few bad primes necessary': each extra place with beta_v non-zero lets
    the reciprocity sum be balanced away from the critical place.

    BUT THE PROPOSED SHORTCUT DOES NOT WORK, and the reason is logical, not
    computational.  beta_p != 0 is a LOCAL fact about the form on W_p;
    'E_d(Q) fails to surject onto W_p' is a GLOBAL fact about one twist.
    The implication runs one way:
        beta_p != 0  AND  beta_v = 0 for v != p   ==>   image is isotropic
                                                   ==>   dim <= 1, no surjection.
    It cannot be inverted: a twist can fail to surject simply because its
    rank is too small, which says nothing about beta_p.  The 708 rank->=2
    twists of the CM search are evidence, not proof, and the document says
    so.

    The valid converse use is a REFUTATION: if a single twist in the class
    DOES span W_p (and has beta_v = 0 away from p), then beta_p vanishes on
    W_p and the mechanism is dead for that class.  So one twist can kill the
    obstruction; no number of twists can establish it.
