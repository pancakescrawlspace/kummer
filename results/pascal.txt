=========================================================================
 Pascal's theorem: one polynomial identity, and what it specializes to
=========================================================================

(1) THE IDENTITY, IN Z[t1,...,t6].

    X = A1A2 ^ A4A5 = [((t4 + t5)*t2 - t5*t4)*t1 - t5*t4*t2, t2*t1 - t5*t4, t1 + (t2 + (-t4 - t5))]
    Y = A2A3 ^ A5A6 = [((t5 + t6)*t3 - t6*t5)*t2 - t6*t5*t3, t3*t2 - t6*t5, t2 + (t3 + (-t5 - t6))]
    Z = A3A4 ^ A6A1 = [((t4 - t6)*t3 - t6*t4)*t1 + t6*t4*t3, -t6*t1 + t4*t3, -t1 + (t3 + (t4 - t6))]

    det[X;Y;Z] = 0     identically zero: 1

    So the three points are collinear for EVERY choice of the six
    parameters, over any field: no genericity hypothesis is needed for
    the identity itself, only for the points to be distinct.

(2) DEGENERATIONS, AS SPECIALIZATIONS OF THAT ONE IDENTITY.

    chord(a,a) is the tangent at a, so letting parameters collide turns
    sides into tangents.  Each line below is the same identity, evaluated.

    pentagon   (t6 = t1: side A6A1 -> tangent at A1)       det = 0
    quadrilateral (t6 = t1, t3 = t2: two tangents)         det = 0
    triangle   (t6=t1, t3=t2, t5=t4: three tangents)       det = 0
    all six equal (everything degenerates)                 det = 0

    The pentagon case reads: for A1..A5 on a conic, the points
      A1A2 ^ A4A5,   A2A3 ^ A5A1,   A3A4 ^ (tangent at A1)
    are collinear.  The triangle case is the classical statement that a
    triangle inscribed in a conic is in perspective with the triangle of
    tangents at its vertices.

(3) PAPPUS = PASCAL FOR A DEGENERATE CONIC (two lines).

    A1,A3,A5 on the line y = 0 and A2,A4,A6 on x = 0, symbolically:
      X = [(-a3*b1 + a3*b2)*a1, b2*b1*a1 - a3*b2*b1, b2*a1 - a3*b1]
      det[X;Y;Z] = 0    identically zero: 1

(5) THE SYNTHETIC PROOF OF SECTION 5 OF THE NOTE, STEP BY STEP.

    (S1) The pencil of lines at the point with parameter u is
         chord(u,t) = [1,-u,0] + t*[0,-1,u],  affine-linear in t, hence a
         projective parametrization of the pencil by t.  Difference:
           chord(u,t) - ([1,-u,0] + t*[0,-1,u]) = [0, 0, 0]
         So the cross-ratio of four such lines is that of the four
         parameters, INDEPENDENT of u: that is Chasles' theorem, and it is
         what makes the two pencils correspond projectively.

    The projectivity m -> p sends  X|->Y,  A4|->V,  A5|->A5,  U|->A6,
    so by (S2) the three joins are concurrent.  Symbolically:
      join(A4,V) is the line A3A4      : 1
      join(U,A6) is the line A6A1      : 1
      the three joins are concurrent   : 1
      and their common point is Z      : 1
    The last two lines are the theorem: the centre of the perspectivity is
    Z = A3A4 ^ A6A1, and the join of X and Y passes through it.

      -- the three joins of corresponding points all pass through Z.

(6) THE HEXAGRAMMUM MYSTICUM.

    Six points carry 6!/(6*2) = 60 hexagons, hence 60 Pascal lines.
    Taking t = [1/2, -3, 5/3, -1/4, 4, -2/5] on the conic xz = y^2:

      hexagons                     : 60
      with non-collinear triple    : 0
      distinct Pascal lines        : 60
      crossings of exactly 2 lines : 1260
      points on exactly 3 lines    : 80    (classically 20 Steiner + 60 Kirkman)
      points on exactly 4 lines    : 45
      check  1260 + 3*80 + 6*45 = 1770 = binomial(60,2) = 1770
      pairs of disjoint chords     : 45
      those ARE the 4-fold points  : 1
