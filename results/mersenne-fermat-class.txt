  (7) the class number formula, and where each field spends it
      p=3    h(+)=1    R=2.77         h*R=2.8           h(-M_p)=1        ratio 2.77   sqrt(M)=3
      p=5    h(+)=1    R=8.02         h*R=8.0           h(-M_p)=3        ratio 2.67   sqrt(M)=6

      p=17   h(+)=9    R=53.27        h*R=479.4         h(-M_p)=285      ratio 1.68   sqrt(M)=362
      p=19   h(+)=1    R=1420.63      h*R=1420.6        h(-M_p)=255      ratio 5.57   sqrt(M)=724
      p=31   h(+)=5    R=15486.21     h*R=77431.1       h(-M_p)=19865    ratio 3.90   sqrt(M)=46341
      h(+) runs 1,1,1,1,9,1,5 while h(-M_p) runs 1,3,5,55,285,255,19865;
      h*R and h(-M_p) are the same size throughout.  h(+) > 1 happens
      exactly where the regulator is anomalously SMALL (p=17: R=53 against
      sqrt(M)=362), never where it is large (p=19: R=1421 > 724, h=1).

Two different phenomena, both real.  For Mersenne discriminants the shape
of the number forces a large cyclic subgroup of the class group, by an
elementary norm computation.  For Fermat discriminants the shape instead
makes the residue symbols that govern the 2-class group explicit, so the
4-rank and 8-rank become computable conditions on k.  Both match the shape
of the Sha question they were a comment on: what the special form of the
prime gives you is the STRUCTURE, and never the SIZE.
