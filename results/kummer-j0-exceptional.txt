  the symbol (g,h) over K is ramified along 3 of the K-divisors,
  and the corestriction A is unramified along ALL sixteen curves : YES

The residues of (g,h) in closed form, checked against the hand values:

      F_[1, 2] : -(r_1-r_0)(s_2-s_0) = 27          agrees
      F_[0, 1] : f'(r_0) = 3 r_0^2 = 9u            agrees
      F_[1, 0] : f~'(s_0) = 3 s_0^2 = 27u^2        agrees
      F_[1, 1] : -(r_1-r_0)(s_1-s_0) = 9(1-z)^2    agrees
  0 of 4 closed forms wrong

  so the two Q-divisors that carry ramification are cleared differently:
    D = orbit of (0,0), k(D) = K : residues 1 and 27, and the second is
        corestricted by N_{L/K}, giving 27^2 = 729 -- a NORM kills it;
    D = orbit of (0,1), k(D) = L : residues 9u, 27u^2, 9(1-z)^2 with
        k(D') = k(D) so cor is the identity, and the PRODUCT is
        9*27*9*u^3*(1-z)^2 = 6561(1-z)^2 = (81(1-z))^2 -- cancellation.

Cross-check.  Over L the corestriction becomes the sum of the three
conjugate symbols, A^(2k,k) for k = 0,1,2 -- the three psi-matched pairs,
which are exactly the (i,j) with i + j = 0 mod 3.  Their product must be
a square in L along every one of the sixteen curves:

  0 of 16 fail over L

Both routes agree.  The point is that (g,h) alone does NOT work: it is
ramified on six of the sixteen curves, and what removes the ramification
is the corestriction -- by a norm from a quadratic extension on one
Q-divisor, and by cancellation among the three conjugates on the other.
