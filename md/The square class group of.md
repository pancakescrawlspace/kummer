# The square class group of $\mathbb{Q}_4$

**Question.** Let $\mathbb{Q}_4$ be the unique unramified quadratic extension of $\mathbb{Q}_2$. Determine $\mathbb{Q}_4^\times/(\mathbb{Q}_4^\times)^2$.

**Answer.** $\mathbb{Q}_4^\times/(\mathbb{Q}_4^\times)^2 \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^4$, of order $16$.

## Setting up

Let $K=\mathbb{Q}_4$, with residue field $\mathbb{F}_4$. Since $K/\mathbb{Q}_2$ is unramified, $2$ is still a uniformizer, and
$$\mathcal{O}_K=\mathbb{Z}_2[\zeta],\qquad \zeta^2+\zeta+1=0,$$
where $\zeta$ is a lift of a generator of $\mathbb{F}_4^\times$.

### Multiplicative structure

Every $2$-adic field decomposes as
$$K^\times \cong \pi^{\mathbb{Z}}\times \mu_{q-1}\times U_1,\qquad U_1=1+\mathfrak{m}_K,$$
where $q=4$ here, so $\mu_{q-1}=\mu_3\cong\mathbb{Z}/3$ and $U_1$ is a pro-$2$ group.

By the structure theorem for principal units of a local field,
$$U_1\cong \mu_{2^\infty}(K)\times \mathbb{Z}_2^{\,n},\qquad n=[K:\mathbb{Q}_2]=2.$$

Now $K$ contains no primitive $4$th root of unity: that would force $K=\mathbb{Q}_2(i)$, which is ramified, not $K$. Hence $\mu_{2^\infty}(K)=\{\pm 1\}$ and
$$U_1\cong \mathbb{Z}/2\times \mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2 .$$

## Passing to squares

Squaring acts as follows on each factor:

| factor | contribution mod squares |
|---|---|
| $\pi^{\mathbb{Z}}\cong\mathbb{Z}$ | $\mathbb{Z}/2$ |
| $\mu_3$ | trivial (odd order, squaring is a bijection) |
| $\mu_2\subset U_1$ | $\mathbb{Z}/2$ |
| $\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2\subset U_1$ | $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$ |

Therefore
$$K^\times/(K^\times)^2 \;\cong\; \underbrace{\mathbb{Z}/2}_{\pi=2}\;\times\;\underbrace{\mathbb{Z}/2}_{-1}\;\times\;\underbrace{(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2}_{U_1/\mu_2}\;=\;(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^4 .$$

This matches the general formula for a $2$-adic field $K$ with $[K:\mathbb{Q}_2]=n$:
$$\bigl|K^\times/(K^\times)^2\bigr| = 2^{\,n+2}\cdot\bigl|\mu_2(K)\bigr| / 2 \;=\; 2^{\,n}\cdot\bigl|\mu_2(K)\bigr|\cdot 2 ,$$
which for $n=2$, $|\mu_2(K)|=2$ gives $16$.

## Explicit representatives

A convenient set of four independent generators, so that all $16$ classes are products of subsets:
$$\{\,2,\;\; -1,\;\; 1+2\zeta,\;\; 1+4\zeta\,\},\qquad \zeta^2+\zeta+1=0 .$$

- $2$ detects the valuation mod $2$.
- $-1$ generates the $2$-torsion of $U_1$.
- $1+2\zeta$ and $-1$ give independent classes modulo $U_2=1+4\mathcal{O}_K$: they reduce to a basis $\{1,\zeta\}$ of $\mathbb{F}_4 = U_1/U_2$.
- $1+4\zeta$ generates the extra $\mathbb{Z}/2$ coming from $U_2/U_1^2$. One checks $(1+2\zeta)^2=-3$ and $U_1^2\cap U_2=\{1,-3\}\cdot U_3$, so $1+4\zeta$ is not congruent to a square.

## Sanity check

Note $\mathbb{Q}_4=\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-3})=\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt 5)$. So $-3$ and $5$, which are nontrivial classes in
$$\mathbb{Q}_2^\times/(\mathbb{Q}_2^\times)^2\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^3 = \langle -1,\,2,\,5\rangle,$$
become **trivial** in $K^\times/(K^\times)^2$. The image of $\mathbb{Q}_2^\times$ therefore drops from order $8$ to order $4$, generated by $-1$ and $2$. The two extra classes $1+2\zeta$ and $1+4\zeta$ come genuinely from $K\setminus\mathbb{Q}_2$, bringing the total rank back up to $4$.
