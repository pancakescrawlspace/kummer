=========================================================================
 Elliptic curves E/Q and primes p with E(Q) non-dense in E(Q_p)
=========================================================================

(1) The filtration, and where density can fail.
    E(Q_p) > E_0(Q_p) > E_1(Q_p) > E_2(Q_p) > ...
    E/E_0 = Phi(F_p), order c_p ;  E_0/E_1 = smooth special fibre ;
    E_1 = formal group = Z_p for p >= 3, with E_n <-> p^(n-1) Z_p.
    The closure of E(Q) is a CLOSED SUBGROUP; if rank >= 1 it is open,
    so 'dense' is the statement that a computable finite index is 1.

    a finite check on a group of order p * |E(Q_p)/E_1(Q_p)|.
    Reduction mod p is NOT enough; one needs mod p^2.

(9) How often does this happen?  Conductor 37, generator P = (0,0).
    The reduction layer alone is cheap to sweep, since it only needs the
    order of Pbar in the finite group E(F_p).

      good p < 5000 : 667
      of these, E(Q) -> E(F_p) NOT surjective : 357   (53.5 %)
      of these, E(F_p) non-cyclic (surjectivity impossible) : 113   (16.9 %)

      So non-density is not exotic: it happens for a large positive
      proportion of p.  The density of p for which a fixed point of
      infinite order generates E(F_p) is the elliptic analogue of Artin's
      primitive-root problem (Lang-Trotter); the observed 46.5 % here is
      the complementary proportion.

(10) The degenerate case: rank 0.  Then E(Q) is finite while E(Q_p) is
     infinite (it contains E_1(Q_p) = Z_p), so the image is never dense,
     at EVERY prime, for trivial reasons.

      E : y^2 + y = x^3 - x^2 - 10x - 20   (conductor 11)
      rank = 0,  E(Q) = 5 points (torsion Z/5)
      E(Q_7) contains E_1(Q_7) = Z_7, uncountable; the image of the 5
      rational points is finite, hence nowhere dense.  Interesting examples
      need rank >= 1, which is why every curve above has rank exactly 1.
